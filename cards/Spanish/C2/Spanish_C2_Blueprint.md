# Spanish (Latin American Standard) — C2 Sentence-Mining Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

**Target total: 3,400 sentences, in 46 batches (45 content batches + 1 mixed-review/integration batch).**

Justification:
- **Vocabulary**: C2 has no fixed lemma ceiling in CEFR terms — the descriptor targets near-native comprehensive range, including low-frequency/specialized vocabulary, full idiomatic and connotative competence, and productive command of archaism, neologism, and register-marked variants. Realistic sentence-mining coverage at this level targets breadth of *usage patterns* over raw new-word count: roughly 2,600–2,800 sentences are needed to cover the long tail of collocation, connotation, and genre-specific phraseology that C2 requires, since most core lemmas were already introduced by C1 and are now being refined for nuance rather than learned from scratch.
- **Grammar/syntax load**: There is essentially no new core grammar at C2; the entire load is pragmatic, stylistic, and connotative mastery — irony, register manipulation, dialectal/sociolectal awareness, archaic and literary forms, and the ability to consciously deploy "incorrect" or marked forms for effect. This is precisely the kind of density the design principles flag as needing dedicated batches rather than incidental coverage.
- **Extra density**: distinguishing near-synonyms by connotation, sarcasm/irony detection, and full sociolinguistic register control (formal archaic to vulgar-informal, all standard-anchored) require large, tightly focused lexical-cluster and pragmatic-contrast batches.
- 3,400 ÷ 46 batches gives batches of ~55–80 sentences, within the clean-generation window.

## 3. Linguistic Justification

**Connotation-over-denotation precision** (Batches 2–3) is the defining C2 task: at this level nearly every lemma a learner needs already exists somewhere in their repertoire, so the remaining work is discriminating among near-synonyms by register, emotional loading, and collocational restriction — a purely usage-based competence that requires large contrastive sentence sets rather than any new rule.

**Regional/dialectal mastery batches** (10–13) are proportionally large because "Latin American Standard" is itself an idealized composite; true C2 competence requires both productive command of the neutral standard and receptive/contextual awareness of major national varieties (voseo pragmatics in the Southern Cone, Caribbean phonological spelling representations, Mexican/Central American discourse markers) — without this, learners plateau at a "textbook neutral" register that doesn't match authentic native input.

**Irony, implicature, and pragmatic batches** (6–7, 30) reflect that C2's core challenge is no longer syntax but pragmatics: understanding what is meant versus what is said, and calibrating politeness/face-saving strategies according to Latin American cultural norms rather than transferring L1 pragmatic defaults.

**Register-transformation batches** (37–38) are a deliberate capstone design choice: taking identical propositional content and mapping it across the full formal-informal and cross-domain spectrum is the most direct test of whether a learner has internalized register as a controllable variable rather than a fixed association with specific vocabulary — this is precisely what separates C2 from C1 productive competence.

**Native-deviation awareness** (Batch 43) — covering well-documented, widespread phenomena like laísmo or dequeísmo — is included for receptive/analytical competence only (per design principle of teaching the standard), so learners can recognize and understand authentic native speech and writing without being taught to produce non-standard forms themselves.

**Long-sentence mastery at literary/legal extremes** (32–33) pushes syntactic embedding to its practical ceiling in Spanish, since C2 candidates must be able to parse (and on demand produce) the most syntactically demanding authentic text types without simplification — the final proof of complete structural command.
