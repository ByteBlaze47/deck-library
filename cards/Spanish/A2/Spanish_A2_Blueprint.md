# Spanish (Latin American Standard) — A2 Sentence-Mining Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

**Target total: 1,250 sentences, in 28 batches (27 content batches + 1 mixed-review/integration batch).**

Justification:
- **Vocabulary**: A2 adds roughly 500–600 new lemmas on top of A1 (cumulative ~1,000–1,150, per PCIC/CEFR A2 benchmarks). With denser sentences now carrying subordinate clauses, ~1.5–2 new/recycled lemmas per sentence and 3–5 exposures per lemma requires ~1,000 sentences for vocabulary alone.
- **Grammar/syntax load**: A2 is where Spanish syntax genuinely opens up — the past-tense split (preterite vs. imperfect), the introduction of subjunctive triggers, full object-pronoun combinations, and future/conditional morphology all appear here. This is the single heaviest grammatical jump in the whole CEFR ladder for Spanish, because aspectual distinction (preterite/imperfect) has no direct English equivalent and requires dozens of contrastive-pair sentences to internalize, not just isolated drills.
- **Extra density**: pronoun-combination word order (se lo, me lo, te la), por/para full contrast, and the earliest subjunctive triggers all demand dedicated batches plus recurring reinforcement, pushing the total above a "vocabulary-only" estimate.
- 1,250 ÷ 28 batches gives batches of ~35–50 sentences, within the clean-generation window.

## 3. Linguistic Justification

**The preterite/imperfect aspectual split** is the defining structural challenge of A2 Spanish and has no direct mapping in English (which uses a single simple past for both). Rather than one contrastive batch, the blueprint spreads this across four connected batches (2, 3, 4, 5) — habitual past, punctual past, direct contrast in narrative, and irregular-verb reinforcement — because learners need repeated narrative-level exposure, not a single grammar rule, to internalize aspect selection.

**Clitic pronoun stacking** (Batches 6–8) is trained as a staged sequence — direct object alone, indirect object alone, then combined se lo/se la — because attempting the combined form too early causes systematic transfer errors (e.g., producing "le lo" instead of "se lo"), a well-documented Spanish-specific error pattern.

**Subjunctive onset** (Batches 22–23, 26) is deliberately restricted at A2 to the most regular, highest-frequency triggers (querer que, es importante que, ojalá, cuando + future reference) rather than the full subjunctive system, which belongs at B1. This mirrors how the mood is genuinely acquired — through fixed, memorizable trigger phrases before productive rule generalization — and avoids diluting A2 output with premature complexity.

**Por/para** (Batch 20) is isolated as its own dedicated batch because it is the single most commonly cited "invisible" error source for English speakers: both prepositions can translate as "for," but the underlying case-like semantic split (cause vs. purpose, origin vs. destination) requires an entire batch of minimal contrastive pairs to overwrite English L1 transfer.

**Command register split** (Batches 12–13) mirrors the A1 tú/usted map but now covers the full negative/affirmative paradigm, since negative commands use subjunctive-derived forms — an early, low-stakes entry point into subjunctive morphology before it's formally introduced as a mood in Batches 22–23.
