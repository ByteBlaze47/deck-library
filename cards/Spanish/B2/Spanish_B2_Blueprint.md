# Spanish (Latin American Standard) — B2 Sentence-Mining Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

**Target total: 2,300 sentences, in 38 batches (37 content batches + 1 mixed-review/integration batch).**

Justification:
- **Vocabulary**: B2 adds ~1,200–1,500 new lemmas (cumulative ~3,500–4,000, per PCIC/CEFR B2 benchmarks), spanning abstract/academic vocabulary, idiomatic expressions, and domain-specific registers (legal, economic, scientific, cultural criticism). Sentence density is higher (multi-clause, idiomatic), so ~1,700–1,800 sentences are needed for cumulative vocabulary coverage alone.
- **Grammar/syntax load**: B2 completes the subjunctive tense system (all sequence-of-tense combinations, including perfect subjunctive forms), formalizes hypothetical/contrary-to-fact conditionals, introduces complex relative structures (cuyo, lo cual), and demands full command of nominalization and stylistic register control. These are subsystems requiring dedicated, recursive batches — adding 500+ sentences beyond vocabulary-only need.
- **Extra density**: idiomatic/fixed-expression clusters and academic discourse cohesion markers are disproportionately dense at B2 and require dedicated lexical-cluster batches, per the design principle for language-specific difficulty.
- 2,300 ÷ 38 batches gives batches of ~45–65 sentences, within the clean-generation window.

## 3. Linguistic Justification

**Full sequence-of-tense subjunctive mastery** (Batches 2–5) is the defining B2 achievement: learners must now select among four subjunctive tense/aspect combinations (present, present perfect, imperfect, pluperfect) based on the temporal relationship to the main clause. This is a genuinely combinatorial system — not additive vocabulary — so the blueprint isolates each new subjunctive tense before integrating all conditional types together in Batch 4, then stress-tests with mixed conditionals in Batch 5, which are a well-known high-difficulty structure even for near-native speakers.

**Cuyo and lo cual/lo que** (Batches 6–7) are isolated because they represent structurally unfamiliar relativization strategies for English speakers: cuyo agrees with the possessed noun rather than the possessor (unlike English "whose"), and lo cual/lo que refer to an entire preceding clause rather than a single noun — both are frequent sources of miscalculation without dedicated contrastive practice.

**Idiomatic and lexical-cluster batches** (12–13, 26–28) are elevated in density at B2 specifically because this is the level where native-like naturalness depends less on new grammar and more on collocational competence — knowing that Spanish says "tener ganas de" rather than a literal translation of "to feel like." Per the design principle, these are treated as dedicated dense clusters rather than incidental vocabulary.

**Register-specific batches** (18–21, 35) reflect that B2 is the level at which learners must produce domain-appropriate text (legal, journalistic, economic, scientific) rather than only general conversation — a requirement that is specific to the productive demands of the B2 descriptor and to how strongly Spanish marks register through both vocabulary and syntactic choices (passive/se-constructions in journalism and law being a clear example).

**Long-sentence parsing and discourse-cohesion batches** (24–25, 31) directly train the long-distance dependency resolution (anaphora, ellipsis, nested subordination) that authentic B2-level Spanish text requires, moving learners beyond sentence-level competence toward paragraph-level coherence.
