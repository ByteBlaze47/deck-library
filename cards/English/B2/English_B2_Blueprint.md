# English (British RP) — B2 Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

B2 adds approximately **1,500–2,000 new headwords** (cumulative ≈ 4,000–5,000) and shifts focus from core grammar acquisition to **stylistic and structural flexibility** — inversion, cleft sentences, participle clauses, and nuanced modality. Because these structures are largely optional/stylistic rather than obligatory, more sentence exposure is needed for learners to internalise *when*, not just *how*, to use them.

- **32 batches**
- **45 sentences per batch**
- **Total: 1,440 sentences**

## 3. Linguistic Justification

B2's central trap is that English marks emphasis and information structure **through word order rather than intonation or particles** (unlike many languages that use discourse particles). Batches 4–5 and 21 isolate **inversion, clefting, and fronting**, since these require the auxiliary to move before the subject in ways that feel deeply unnatural to learners used to fixed SVO order.

**Participle clauses** (batch 9) are flagged separately because they require correctly identifying the implied (often unstated) subject — a major source of "dangling participle" errors even among advanced learners.

The **subjunctive** (batch 11) is a fossil form in modern English, surviving only in formal *that*-clauses after suggestion/necessity verbs — it must be taught as an isolated exception rather than a productive rule.

At this level, RP pronunciation instruction shifts toward **contrastive/emphatic stress patterns** (nuclear stress placement for cleft and inverted sentences) and **register-appropriate intonation** — the falling, measured pitch pattern typically associated with formal RP delivery — which is why formal/informal register is explicitly contrasted in batches 19–20.
