# English (British RP) — A1 Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

Based on the English Vocabulary Profile (British Council / Cambridge), the A1 band covers roughly **550–750 headwords** and a small, closed set of core grammatical structures (the verb *to be*, present simple, basic question forms, articles, plurals, simple prepositions of time/place, and the two most frequent past forms). To give each lexical item enough contextual exposure (minimum 3–5 sentence contacts per item across different structures) while keeping batches short enough to avoid AI output truncation, the curriculum is fixed at:

- **20 batches**
- **30 sentences per batch**
- **Total: 600 sentences**

This figure sits deliberately above raw vocabulary count to allow each grammar point to recur across multiple thematic contexts (recycling), which is essential at A1 since learners have no fallback grammatical intuition yet.

## 3. Linguistic Justification

English carries almost no morphological case marking, so at A1 the single greatest structural trap is **rigid SVO word order** — meaning is signalled by position, not by inflection, which is counter-intuitive for learners coming from case-marking L1s. Batches 1–6 therefore isolate word order before any tense variation is introduced.

The **verb *to be* vs *do*-support split** (batches 1–2, 9, 19) is unique to English among many languages: learners must learn early that *be* stands alone (*Is she happy?*) while all other verbs require an auxiliary (*Does she like tea?*). Separating these into distinct batches prevents cross-contamination errors like *"Does she is happy?"*.

**Article usage (a/an/the/zero)** is flagged as a standalone focus (batch 6) because English articles have no equivalent in many L1s and cannot be taught by direct translation — they require repeated pattern exposure.

Finally, because the target variety is **Received Pronunciation**, sentence content is lexically controlled to foreground RP-relevant phonemes from the outset: non-rhotic endings (*car*, *father*), the TRAP–BATH vowel split (*class*, *dance*, *bath*), and weak-form function words (*a*, *to*, *of*, *the*) that carry English's stress-timed rhythm. Batches are kept short (30 sentences) specifically so pronunciation drilling of these features can happen sentence-by-sentence without cognitive overload.
