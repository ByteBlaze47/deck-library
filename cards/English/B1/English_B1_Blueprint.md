# English (British RP) — B1 Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

B1 adds roughly **1,000–1,200 new headwords** (cumulative ≈ 2,500–2,900) and is where English's most structurally irregular systems appear together: passive voice, reported speech, non-defining relative clauses, and mixed conditional/modal-perfect forms. Because several of these systems interact (e.g. passive + reported speech), batch size is increased to give more recombination practice per structure.

- **28 batches**
- **40 sentences per batch**
- **Total: 1,120 sentences**

## 3. Linguistic Justification

The dominant trap at B1 is the **backshift rule in reported speech** (batches 8–9): English shifts tense one step into the past inside the reported clause (*"I am tired" → She said she was tired*), a rule with no direct equivalent in many languages and one that interacts awkwardly with modals, which don't always backshift regularly (*must* often stays *must*).

**Passive voice formation** (batches 6–7) is isolated because English passives require exact auxiliary+past-participle agreement across all tense/aspect combinations, and errors compound quickly once perfect or modal passives are attempted without prior simple-passive mastery.

The **defining vs non-defining relative clause distinction** (batches 10–11) hinges entirely on punctuation and intonation (comma pause in speech) rather than any lexical marker — a purely prosodic cue that RP speakers signal through a distinct falling pitch contour around the inserted clause, making spoken drilling essential here.

Finally, B1 introduces **register variation** (batch 25) as RP itself is strongly associated with formal/neutral registers; learners need explicit contrastive sentences to recognise when contracted, colloquial forms are appropriate versus when full forms are expected in careful RP speech.
