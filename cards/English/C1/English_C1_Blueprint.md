# English (British RP) — C1 Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

C1 adds approximately **2,500–3,000 new headwords** (cumulative ≈ 7,000–8,000, including academic and low-frequency vocabulary) and centres on **discourse-level competence**: managing register, stance, cohesion, and rhetorical structure across whole texts rather than isolated sentences. Batch size increases again to accommodate the longer, multi-clause sentences typical of this level.

- **36 batches**
- **50 sentences per batch**
- **Total: 1,800 sentences**

## 3. Linguistic Justification

C1's defining trap is that grammatical accuracy is no longer the limiting factor — **pragmatic and stylistic appropriateness** is. Batches 6, 18, and 33 isolate **modality and stance markers**, because English distinguishes fine gradients of certainty (*may/might/could/is likely to/appears to*) that map to different levels of authorial commitment, a distinction easily flattened by learners into a single "maybe."

**Register shifting** (batch 13) is treated as its own structural skill rather than vocabulary choice, since the same proposition must be re-encoded through entirely different syntax depending on context (e.g. legal *"the party shall..."* vs conversational *"you have to..."*).

**Irony and understatement** (batch 29) are flagged specifically because RP-associated British discourse conventions rely heavily on litotes and downplaying (*"not bad"* meaning *very good*) — a pragmatic layer that sentence-level grammar training must explicitly model, since literal translation fails entirely here.

Phonologically, C1 sentence sets are curated to reinforce **RP's characteristic intonational restraint** — narrower pitch range, controlled nuclear stress placement for irony/emphasis — since misplaced stress can invert intended meaning (e.g. sarcastic vs sincere *"how nice"*).
