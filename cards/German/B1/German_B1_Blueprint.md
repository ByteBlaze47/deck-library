# German (Standard High German) — B1 Sentence-Based Curriculum Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

B1 targets roughly **2,000–2,500 active/passive vocabulary items** and marks the shift into independent, connected discourse: hypothetical reasoning, reported speech, and the full passive-voice paradigm. Estimated requirement:

**≈ 1,230 sentences**, divided into 18 topic batches.

Growth here is driven less by raw vocabulary and more by the need to drill each new mood/voice construction (Konjunktiv II, Konjunktiv I, Zustandspassiv) across enough varied contexts to become productive rather than merely recognizable.

## 3. Linguistic Justification

- **Konjunktiv II**: a full irrealis mood system with its own conjugation set, required for hypotheticals and politeness — a major conceptual leap from indicative-only A2 (Batches 3–4).
- **Konjunktiv I**: formal indirect speech uses a separate, often archaic-feeling conjugation, frequently substituted by Konjunktiv II in speech — both must be recognized (Batch 5).
- **Zustandspassiv vs. Vorgangspassiv**: `sein`-passive (state) vs. `werden`-passive (process) is a distinction with no direct English parallel and is a common B1 error source (Batch 6).
- **`je...desto` comparative clauses**: require inverted word order in both clauses simultaneously, a syntactic pattern unique to this construction (Batch 15).
- **Partizip as adjective**: participial adjectives can carry their own extended modifiers before the noun (erweiterte Partizipialattribute), foreshadowing B2/C1 nominal-style density (Batch 16).
