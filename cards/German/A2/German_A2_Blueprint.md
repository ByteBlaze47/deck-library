# German (Standard High German) — A2 Sentence-Based Curriculum Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

A2 expands active vocabulary to roughly **1,300 words** (Goethe-Institut A2 wordlist) and introduces past-tense narration, comparison, adjective declension, and the first subordinating connectors beyond `weil`/`dass`. Estimated requirement:

**≈ 970 sentences**, divided into 16 topic batches.

The increase over A1 reflects not just new vocabulary but the combinatorial explosion of adjective endings (which now vary by case, gender, and article type) and the coexistence of Perfekt and Präteritum for narration.

## 3. Linguistic Justification

- **Adjective declension explosion**: unlike A1's mostly predicative adjectives, A2 requires attributive adjectives to agree with case, gender, and number, with a different ending set depending on whether a definite, indefinite, or no article precedes it (Batch 5).
- **Präteritum vs. Perfekt coexistence**: written narration favors Präteritum while spoken German favors Perfekt, so learners must recognize both simultaneously (Batch 2).
- **Passive voice**: the `werden`-passive reorders the semantic agent out of subject position, a conceptual shift from the active-only A1 stage (Batch 12).
- **Relative clauses**: relative pronouns mirror the definite article but trigger verb-final order, compounding the case system with subordinate-clause syntax (Batch 14).
- **Genitive case**: increasingly rare in spoken German but essential for formal register, introduced late once nominative/accusative/dative are stable (Batch 16).
