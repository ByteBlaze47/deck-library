# German (Standard High German) — B2 Sentence-Based Curriculum Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

B2 targets roughly **4,000 vocabulary items**, with a shift toward abstract, argumentative, and professional registers. The dominant challenge is no longer new grammar rules but **stylistic control**: nominal style, extended attributes, and register-appropriate connectors. Estimated requirement:

**≈ 1,160 sentences**, divided into 18 topic batches.

## 3. Linguistic Justification

- **Nominal style (Nominalstil)**: formal/academic German prefers noun-heavy constructions over verb chains (`nach Beendigung der Arbeit` vs. `nachdem die Arbeit beendet wurde`), a register shift with no direct English parallel (Batch 1).
- **Extended participial attributes**: entire modifying phrases are compressed before the noun they modify (`der von der Regierung geplante Gesetzentwurf`), requiring the reader to hold the noun in suspension until the end — a genuine parsing challenge (Batch 5).
- **`sein` + `zu` + infinitive**: a passive-modal hybrid (`Das Problem ist zu lösen` = "must be solved") that condenses meaning atypically compactly (Batch 2).
- **Modal particles**: words like `ja`, `doch`, `eben`, `halt` carry no lexical meaning but shift pragmatic tone, a purely register-level skill (Batch 13).
- **Causative `lassen`**: structurally similar to English "have something done" but with distinct case and word-order rules (Batch 12).
