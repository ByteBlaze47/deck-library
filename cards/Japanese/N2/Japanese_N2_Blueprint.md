**1. Total Scope Assessment**

Target total: **4,200–4,800 sentences**.

Justification:  
JLPT N2 requires roughly 6,000 vocabulary items (cumulative; approximately 2,000–2,250 new beyond N3) and 150–200+ grammar/syntactic patterns. The defining leap is into upper-intermediate formal and written registers: dense formal connectors and set phrases (にあたって, をめぐって, に際して, に関して, etc.), fine-grained cause/contrast/addition (だけに, ばかりか, どころか, にもかかわらず, うえに), stronger modality and epistemic judgment (に違いない, かねない, とは限らない, にすぎない), full contextual keigo, abstract nominalizations, and multi-clause argumentative discourse typical of news, opinion, and workplace texts.  

These introduce longer left-branching structures, subtle speaker attitude distinctions, and register shifts that are far more demanding than N3. High-density sentence mining therefore needs multiple controlled embeddings (typically 5–8 sentences per major structure) inside coherent situations, progressive lengthening of formal and analytical sentences, and distributed recycling of the large abstract vocabulary load. 4,200–4,800 sentences deliver thorough coverage of the standard N2 inventory while remaining practical for clean AI generation in tightly controlled batches and for deliberate exposure cycles. This range prevents under-exposure of the hardest N2 traps (near-synonym distinctions, formal written patterns, keigo in extended discourse, long-sentence parsing) without creating diluted or overly long batches.

**3. Linguistic Justification**

Japanese (Tokyo Standard) at N2 is the level at which the language shifts decisively into adult formal and written registers:

- **Formal occasion and topic-framing patterns**: にあたって, に際して, をめぐって, に関して, etc., encode procedural and discursive framing that appears constantly in notices, news, and workplace Japanese. Isolated study fails; thematic batches embed them inside realistic formal situations so the learner experiences their natural distribution and collocations.
- **Fine-shaded cause, contrast, and addition**: Near-synonyms such as だけに / ばかりに, ばかりか / どころか, にもかかわらず / ものの carry distinct speaker attitudes and expectedness. Dedicated sequential batches force discrimination of nuance rather than treating them as interchangeable “because / although / moreover.”
- **Epistemic and deontic modality**: に違いない, かねない, とは限らない, ざるを得ない, ないわけにはいかない express degrees of certainty, risk, and compulsion with greater precision than N3. Controlled exposure trains the subtle differences in speaker commitment.
- **Full contextual keigo**: Beyond basic forms, N2 requires appropriate honorific/humble choices across extended interactions. Interleaving keigo with formal connectors and professional themes builds situational competence.
- **Abstract nominalization and set phrases**: Patterns built on というものだ, ということだ, とされる, and dense Sino-Japanese compounds create the compact, left-branching style of newspaper and analytical Japanese. Progressive density batches prepare the learner for real N2 reading.
- **Discourse architecture**: N2 texts routinely stack relative clauses, logical connectors (したがって, 一方, ところが), and multi-sentence arguments. Later batches deliberately increase sentence length and connector density so the learner masters the characteristic particle-scaffolded, information-heavy syntax of upper-intermediate Tokyo-standard Japanese.

By keeping grammar almost never isolated and distributing the large abstract vocabulary load across situational themes, the blueprint ensures every batch simultaneously advances communicative usefulness and the precise structural traps of N2. The final mixed-review batch forces cumulative recombination—the critical step that converts controlled exposure into robust recognition and production at the threshold of professional Japanese.
