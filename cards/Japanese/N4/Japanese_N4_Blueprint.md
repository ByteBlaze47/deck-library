# \*\*1. Total Scope Assessment\*\*

# 

# Target total: \*\*2,200–2,500 sentences\*\*.

# 

# Justification:  

# JLPT N4 requires roughly 1,500 vocabulary items (cumulative; \~700 new beyond N5) and approximately 130–170 grammar/syntactic patterns. The major additions are potential forms, the four conditionals (たら・ば・と・なら), giving/receiving verbs and their て-forms (あげる・くれる・もらう), volitional forms, early passive/causative, てしまう／ておく／てみる, hearsay/appearance markers (そうだ・ようだ・らしい), simultaneous ながら, transitive/intransitive pairs, and denser discourse connectors.  

# 

# These introduce longer dependency chains, direction-sensitive favor expressions, nuanced conditionality, and register shifts that are far more demanding than N5. High-density sentence mining therefore needs multiple controlled embeddings (typically 4–7 sentences per major structure) inside coherent situations, progressive lengthening of multi-clause sentences, and distributed recycling of the expanded vocabulary. 2,200–2,500 sentences deliver full coverage of the standard N4 inventory while remaining practical for clean AI generation in tightly controlled batches and for deliberate exposure cycles. This range prevents under-exposure of the hardest N4 traps (conditionals, giving/receiving directionality, て-chaining) without creating diluted or overly long batches.

# \*\*3. Linguistic Justification\*\*

# 

# Japanese (Tokyo Standard) at N4 is the first level at which the language’s characteristic systems become truly interlocking and productive:

# 

# \- \*\*Direction-sensitive favor system\*\*: あげる／くれる／もらう (and their て-forms) encode the social direction of benefit with precision that English lacks. Early isolation produces persistent errors; the architecture therefore dedicates sequential batches to objects and then to actions, embedding them inside realistic social situations so the learner internalizes the deictic rules.

# \- \*\*Four conditionals with distinct profiles\*\*: と (natural/inevitable), ば (provisional), たら (versatile if/when), なら (topic-conditional) are not interchangeable. Separate thematic batches force the learner to experience the functional differences rather than treat them as a single “if” category.

# \- \*\*て-form as a productive hub\*\*: Beyond N5 progressive, N4 multiplies the auxiliary load (てみる, ておく, てしまう, てあげる etc.). Dedicated batches train chaining and nuance while thematic embedding keeps the sentences communicatively useful.

# \- \*\*Transitive/intransitive pairs and early voice\*\*: The systematic pairing of 他動詞／自動詞 plus the first passive and causative forms create long-distance argument structure that learners must parse and produce. Explicit contrast batches address this Japanese-specific difficulty.

# \- \*\*Hearsay, appearance, and conjecture layers\*\*: そうだ (two uses), ようだ, らしい, かもしれない, はずだ introduce evidentiality and modality that interact with tense and aspect. Progressive batches prevent confusion between visual appearance and reported information.

# \- \*\*Register and discourse density\*\*: N4 reading and listening already contain multi-clause sentences and polite–plain shifts. Later batches deliberately increase sentence length, compounding density, and connector use so the learner masters the left-branching, particle-scaffolded syntax characteristic of real Tokyo-standard Japanese at this level.

# 

# By keeping grammar almost never isolated and distributing the expanded vocabulary across situational themes, the blueprint ensures every batch simultaneously advances communicative usefulness and the precise structural traps of N4. The final mixed-review batch forces cumulative recombination—the critical step that converts controlled exposure into robust recognition and production.
