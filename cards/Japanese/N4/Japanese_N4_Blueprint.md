# Japanese (Tokyo Standard) — N4 Sentence-Based Curriculum Blueprint

## 1. Total Scope Assessment

N4 raises the cumulative vocabulary target to **~1,500 words** and **~300 kanji**, but the defining feature of this level is the introduction of the **plain form (普通形)** as a grammatical foundation — enabling embedded clauses, casual speech, and the conditional/passive/causative systems. Roughly **100 new grammar patterns** are layered on top of N5.

Because plain-form mastery unlocks compound sentence structures (conditionals, quoting, causative-passive), each new pattern needs deeper contextual repetition than N5's — averaging 4–6 exposures per item.

**Target: ~760 sentences**, organized into **28 batches**, capped at 40 sentences each.

## 3. Linguistic Justification

- **Plain-form dependency chain:** Nearly every N4 grammar point (conditionals, hearsay, embedded opinion) requires the plain form as its base. Batch 1 is deliberately sentence-count-heavy (35) because errors here propagate through the remaining 27 batches.
- **Four overlapping conditionals (と/ば/たら/なら):** English collapses all of these into "if." Japanese assigns each a distinct nuance (habitual, hypothetical, sequential, topic-based) with non-overlapping felicity conditions — hence four separate batches (5–8) rather than one combined "conditionals" batch, to prevent interference errors.
- **Passive/causative morphology stacking:** English marks passive with an auxiliary ("was eaten") and causation with a separate verb ("made him eat"), but Japanese fuses both into verb suffixes that can combine (causative-passive, Batch 11) — a purely agglutinative pattern absent from English morphology.
- **Relative clauses reverse word order:** "The book that I bought" becomes [私が買った本] — modifier-then-noun, verb-final inside the clause. This is consistently the single largest source of N4 comprehension errors for English speakers, justifying the outsized Batch 3 (40 sentences).
- **Emergent keigo (honorific) layer:** N4 introduces the first productive respect/humility verb substitutions (Batches 23–24), a grammatical category with no English equivalent — English encodes politeness lexically/tonally, not morphologically.
